The House of Birdmoney

“Darling, you can’t possibly leave without looking at the earrings.”

Gloria Birdmoney was not born wealthy. She simply arrived that way, and has spent every year since refusing to explain the difference.

Somewhere between three continents and one very contested inheritance, Gloria assembled a personal archive of jewelry, fashion, and rare objects that eventually outgrew her closets, then her manor, then the concept of a closet entirely. What began as acquisition became philosophy: nothing in the Birdmoney collection is bought because it is needed. Everything is bought because it is correct.

About the House

Gloria Birdmoney

“Yes, they’re expensive. No, I don’t see the problem.”

Gloria Birdmoney is, by her own account, “a woman of considerable and largely self-explanatory means.” She has spent more than two decades collecting jewelry, fashion, accessories, and objects she describes only as “important,” assembling a personal archive that eventually became a business, mostly by accident.

She favors gold that has clearly been worked by hand, pearls that refuse to be perfectly round, and velvet in colors that do not appear on standard swatches. She dislikes the word “trend,” the concept of “one nice piece,” and being kept waiting.

Gloria maintains residences she declines to name and a wardrobe she declines to count. She is, by all accounts, difficult to buy for — which is precisely why she started selling instead.
